It may seem that the difference between Nosé-Hoover-Langevin and Langevin
dynamics is a technical one that essentially hinges on choice of parameters. Both
methods are ergodic and it may appear that by simply choosing a small value
of friction in Langevin dynamics, we can match the behavior of Nosé-Hoover-
Langevin to any desired accuracy. However, this is most deﬁnitely not the case, as a
careful analysis of Figs. 8.3 and 8.4 reveals. If an accurate velocity autocorrelation
function is required, then the friction parameter in Langevin dynamics needs to be
chosen very small (much less than 
 D 0:1), see Fig. 8.4. But if the friction is
chosen in this way, the convergence to canonical equilibrium is drastically impaired
(Figs. 8.2 and 8.3). By contrast it is relatively easy to ﬁnd parameter choices
for Nosé-Hoover-Langevin which provide quite rapid convergence of canonical
averages while preserving the accuracy of autocorrelation functions.
