For general systems it can be very challenging to give a complete description of
the weak error in averages computed from trajectories evolved through a Langevin
dynamics discretization scheme. However, using a linear model problem signiﬁ-
cantly reduces the complexity, while using small dimensional test problems allows
us to effectively annihilate sampling error in demonstrative numerical experiments.
As such, we return now to the harmonic oscillator, which as well as being the
simplest molecular model is also one of the most relevant for molecular dynamics
applications, as many issues of stability and timestep in molecular dynamics
simulations arise due to harmonic potentials used to model covalent bonds (such
as in crystalline solids and biomolecules). The canonical distributions of position
and momentum are also of a simple form (Gaussians), making such oscillators
particularly amenable to analysis.
